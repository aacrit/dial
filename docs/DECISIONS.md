## 2026-09-26: Law 2 allows removing a translator's apparatus, by the pinned script only
Founder decision in chat, from the T2 review. A translator's apparatus (footnotes, note markers, editorial marks) may be removed only by `scripts/extract-work.mjs`, which checks the upstream Gutenberg file's SHA-256 before it cuts and refuses unless the note markers match the notes one for one; the Bookplate says what was removed, and every word of the text itself stays verbatim. First use: George Long's Meditations Book II (eBook 15877), without his footnotes, their markers and his + marks, his bracketed words kept.

## 2026-09-26: work_opened counts Tune in on a work
Founder decision in chat, from the T2 review. `work_opened` is sent when Tune in is pressed on a work, once per work per page load; tuning and browsing the dial send nothing. It counts a choice to listen, not a needle passing a station, so the Repertory's kill-criteria reading is not inflated by browsing. design/spec.md section 2's event line says the same.

## 2026-09-27: Law 3 amendment: a Repertory work may carry a cast sheet
Founder decision in chat, after Crito's speaker labels were heard read aloud in one voice. "A Repertory work may carry a cast sheet, written by the curator from the text's own list of persons and shown on the Bookplate, that declares only voice sex; the engine never parses names." Casting lives in `web/src/engine/cast.ts` as `castVoices(speakers, sheet, palette)`, speakers in order of first appearance with their word counts; for now it gives each speaker the first unused voice of the character palette (Fable, then Lewis) that fits the sheet, wrapping only once all are used, and T2c replaces the rule with quality and contrast scoring. George is the male narrator and is never in the character palette (next entry). Crito's sheet, from Gutenberg #1657's list of persons: Socrates and Crito, male voices.

## 2026-09-27: Two fixed narrator voices, never given to characters
Founder rule, for the casting interface (the full algorithm is T2c). Dial has two narrator voices across all works: female `af_heart`, and a male to be chosen by ear, with `bm_george` as its placeholder so the Cave and the Meditations keep their sound. A work's cast sheet declares `narrator: "m" | "f"` beside its speakers' voice sex. The narrator reads every unlabelled line; characters are cast from a palette that excludes both narrator voices, so Crito (a labelled script with no narration, narrator declared male) casts Socrates as `bm_fable` and Crito as `bm_lewis`, Kokoro's two best-graded British male voices after George. `af_heart` is staged and pinned now so T2c can use it.

## 2026-09-27: Law 2 lets the voice leave out a speaker label, and nothing else new
From the T2b review. Law 2 said `spoken` may differ from `text` only by whitespace; a labelled script (Crito's "SOCRATES:  ") had its labels read aloud. `spoken` may now also leave out the one speaker label that opens a turn, as `speakerLabel()` in `web/src/engine/segment.ts` finds it; `text` keeps the label, so every cue is still a byte-exact slice and the cues still rebuild the source. The read-along shows the speaker's name, derived from that label, in place of it. Headings (BOOK, CHAPTER, SCENE and the like), Roman numerals and honorifics with a full stop (MR., ST., NO.) are never labels.

## 2026-09-27: T2c casting: the founder's narrators, one accent per work, a fixed integer score
Founder decisions, from the voice audition and the first T2c cast. The male narrator is `am_michael`, chosen by ear; the female narrator stays `af_heart`, also chosen by ear and kept despite her faster pace (about 173 to 187 wpm as played). Narrator voices are never given to characters; `bm_george`, the placeholder, is an ordinary character voice. Voices are never sped up or slowed down: pace is a casting decision. The cast sheet declares voice sex for the narrator and for every labelled speaker (a sheet that leaves one out is not honoured), plus an optional `accent: "us" | "uk"`, and the engine never parses names.
One accent per work: every character voice shares the sheet's accent, else the narrator's (both narrators are US). A first cast put an American Socrates beside a British Crito; a mixed-accent cast sounds like a mixed production, not one company performing one text, and the listener hears the accent as meaning something it does not. A speaker whose sex has no fitting voice in that accent takes the other accent, alone, and its reason records `accentFallback`; the Bookplate says "in one accent" only when that is true.
The algorithm (`web/src/engine/cast.ts`, header and named constants): the pool is `design/voices.json` without the narrators, hexgrad's published grade C or better (`MIN_GRADE`; D+ only when a declared sex has nothing better), 150 to 215 wpm as played (`af_nicole`, 121, is out), of the work's accent and the declared sex. Speakers ranked by word share each take the unused voice with the highest integer score: grade x share per mille, plus each already-cast speaker's share of the work's exchanges (per mille) x the two voices' contrast / 1000, plus, in a narrated work only, the contrast with the narrator x 250 / 1000. Contrast is a per-mille distance over pitch (cents, weight 5), brightness (spectral centroid, 3) and pace (2); accent is not a dimension. A lead takes the better grade unless the lower voice contrasts by more than a grade step's worth; a minor part is cast for contrast. The most-alternating pair must differ by 200 cents of pitch or 300 Hz of brightness. When voices run out, a speaker shares only with speakers it never directly answers; where it cannot, the report records `clash`. `CAST_ENGINE_VERSION` (`web/src/engine/cast-version.ts`) is "2".
Result: Crito is Socrates `am_fenrir`, Crito `am_puck` (both C+; with no narration their scores tie at 5530 for Socrates and the id order decides); the Cave and the Meditations are read by `am_michael`. Only the narrators and these voices are staged and pinned: `am_michael`, `af_heart`, `am_fenrir`, `am_puck`. The US male pool is only Fenrir and Puck, so a third American man who answers both would clash; the pool limit goes to the founder.

## 2026-09-27: No-scroll panels, and the Seal becomes a widget (G2 amendment)
The founder asked for a retro, no-scroll interface: every room is made of full-screen panels of fitted widgets, switched by a band-selector plate, with no sideways scrolling at any size. The Seal is too technical for a page of its own. It becomes a radio widget (a magic eye, one plain sentence, the counts switch, and "Show every request" collapsed), /seal is removed, and the speed test moves to T7's gauge. Signed off in chat, with three answers: on a phone the script fills its panel when tapped; rooms are switched in the top bar; large screens use one panel when it fits.

## 2026-09-27: A listen counts when 80% of the work is heard (CoS decision A)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T5 review. `chapter_rendered` is sent once per listen when the listener has heard at least 80% of the work's running time, on either path (Dial's prepared recording, or made on this device), counted as seconds of audio played and never as audio seeked over. It no longer fires when the last line is made, nor when a listen reaches the end by a skip. A listen carried on across a restart (Resume at line N, or a recording made on the device after a decode failure) keeps what it had heard and counts once. The privacy page, the Seal's switch and design/spec.md say so ("a work heard for at least 80% of its length"); `web/src/broadcast-state.ts` `heardStep` and `countsAsListen` hold the rule. The kill criterion's `chapter_rendered` count now reads as listens.

## 2026-09-27: A recording this browser cannot decode is made on the device instead (CoS decision B)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T5 review. When a part of Dial's prepared recording fails to decode (an EncodingError, as older iOS Safari may give for Opus in WebM), the listen carries on made on this device from the line on air, with no dead end, and says why: "This browser can't play Dial's recording, so it's being made on this device." The lines before it keep the recording's lengths, so the strip and the script still line up; the file made from that line on is kept for seeking and is not offered as the work's download. For the rest of the visit every work is made on the device. A remuxed, Safari-friendly set of recordings is a follow-up (T5b), not built now.
