## 2026-09-26: Law 2 allows removing a translator's apparatus, by the pinned script only
Founder decision in chat, from the T2 review. A translator's apparatus (footnotes, note markers, editorial marks) may be removed only by `scripts/extract-work.mjs`, which checks the upstream Gutenberg file's SHA-256 before it cuts and refuses unless the note markers match the notes one for one; the Bookplate says what was removed, and every word of the text itself stays verbatim. First use: George Long's Meditations Book II (eBook 15877), without his footnotes, their markers and his + marks, his bracketed words kept.

## 2026-09-27: Law 3 amendment: a Repertory work may carry a cast sheet
Founder decision in chat, after Crito's speaker labels were heard read aloud in one voice. "A Repertory work may carry a cast sheet, written by the curator from the text's own list of persons and shown on the Bookplate, that declares only voice sex; the engine never parses names." Casting lives in `web/src/engine/cast.ts` as `castVoices(speakers, sheet, palette)`, speakers in order of first appearance with their word counts; for now it gives each speaker the first unused voice of the character palette (Fable, then Lewis) that fits the sheet, wrapping only once all are used, and T2c replaces the rule with quality and contrast scoring. George is the male narrator and is never in the character palette (next entry). Crito's sheet, from Gutenberg #1657's list of persons: Socrates and Crito, male voices.

## 2026-09-27: Two fixed narrator voices, never given to characters
Founder rule, for the casting interface (the full algorithm is T2c). Dial has two narrator voices across all works: female `af_heart`, and a male to be chosen by ear, with `bm_george` as its placeholder so the Cave and the Meditations keep their sound. A work's cast sheet declares `narrator: "m" | "f"` beside its speakers' voice sex. The narrator reads every unlabelled line; characters are cast from a palette that excludes both narrator voices, so Crito (a labelled script with no narration, narrator declared male) casts Socrates as `bm_fable` and Crito as `bm_lewis`, Kokoro's two best-graded British male voices after George. `af_heart` is staged and pinned now so T2c can use it.

## 2026-09-27: Law 2 lets the voice leave out a speaker label, and nothing else new
From the T2b review. Law 2 said `spoken` may differ from `text` only by whitespace; a labelled script (Crito's "SOCRATES:  ") had its labels read aloud. `spoken` may now also leave out the one speaker label that opens a turn, as `speakerLabel()` in `web/src/engine/segment.ts` finds it; `text` keeps the label, so every cue is still a byte-exact slice and the cues still rebuild the source. The read-along shows the speaker's name, derived from that label, in place of it. Headings (BOOK, CHAPTER, SCENE and the like), Roman numerals and honorifics with a full stop (MR., ST., NO.) are never labels.

## 2026-09-27: T2c casting: the founder's narrators, one accent per work, a fixed integer score
Founder decisions, from the voice audition and the first T2c cast. The male narrator is `am_michael`, chosen by ear; the female narrator stays `af_heart`, also chosen by ear and kept despite her faster pace (about 173 to 187 wpm as played). Narrator voices are never given to characters; `bm_george`, the placeholder, is an ordinary character voice. Voices are never sped up or slowed down: pace is a casting decision. The cast sheet declares voice sex for the narrator and for every labelled speaker (a sheet that leaves one out is not honoured), plus an optional `accent: "us" | "uk"`, and the engine never parses names.
One accent per work: every character voice shares the sheet's accent, else the narrator's (both narrators are US). A first cast put an American Socrates beside a British Crito; a mixed-accent cast sounds like a mixed production, not one company performing one text, and the listener hears the accent as meaning something it does not. A speaker whose sex has no fitting voice in that accent takes the other accent, alone, and its reason records `accentFallback`; the Bookplate says "in one accent" only when that is true.
The algorithm (`web/src/engine/cast.ts`, header and named constants): the pool is `design/voices.json` without the narrators, hexgrad's published grade C or better (`MIN_GRADE`; D+ only when a declared sex has nothing better), 150 to 215 wpm as played (`af_nicole`, 121, is out), of the work's accent and the declared sex. Speakers ranked by word share each take the unused voice with the highest integer score: grade x share per mille, plus each already-cast speaker's share of the work's exchanges (per mille) x the two voices' contrast / 1000, plus, in a narrated work only, the contrast with the narrator x 250 / 1000. Contrast is a per-mille distance over pitch (cents, weight 5), brightness (spectral centroid, 3) and pace (2); accent is not a dimension. A lead takes the better grade unless the lower voice contrasts by more than a grade step's worth; a minor part is cast for contrast. The most-alternating pair must differ by 200 cents of pitch or 300 Hz of brightness. When voices run out, a speaker shares only with speakers it never directly answers; where it cannot, the report records `clash`. `CAST_ENGINE_VERSION` (`web/src/engine/cast-version.ts`) is "2".
Result: Crito is Socrates `am_fenrir`, Crito `am_puck` (both C+; with no narration their scores tie at 5530 for Socrates and the id order decides); the Cave and the Meditations are read by `am_michael`. Only the narrators and these voices are staged and pinned: `am_michael`, `af_heart`, `am_fenrir`, `am_puck`. The US male pool is only Fenrir and Puck, so a third American man who answers both would clash; the pool limit goes to the founder.

## 2026-09-27: A listen counts when 80% of the work is heard (CoS decision A)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T5 review. `chapter_rendered` is sent once per listen when the listener has heard at least 80% of the work's running time, on either path (Dial's prepared recording, or made on this device), counted as seconds of audio played and never as audio seeked over. It no longer fires when the last line is made, nor when a listen reaches the end by a skip. A listen carried on across a restart (Resume at line N, or a recording made on the device after a decode failure) keeps what it had heard and counts once. The privacy page, the Seal's switch and design/spec.md say so ("a work heard for at least 80% of its length"); `web/src/broadcast-state.ts` `heardStep` and `countsAsListen` hold the rule. The kill criterion's `chapter_rendered` count now reads as listens.

## 2026-09-27: A recording this browser cannot decode is made on the device instead (CoS decision B)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T5 review. When a part of Dial's prepared recording fails to decode (an EncodingError, as older iOS Safari may give for Opus in WebM), the listen carries on made on this device from the line on air, with no dead end, and says why: "This browser can't play Dial's recording, so it's being made on this device." The lines before it keep the recording's lengths, so the strip and the script still line up; the file made from that line on is kept for seeking and is not offered as the work's download. For the rest of the visit every work is made on the device. A remuxed, Safari-friendly set of recordings is a follow-up (T5b), not built now.

## 2026-09-27: Heard means distinct line time (CoS decision C)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T5 re-review. What a listen has heard is kept per line: each stretch of a line counts once, the first time it is played, so a replay adds nothing (`web/src/broadcast-state.ts` `addHeard`, with `heardStep` still refusing seeks and pauses). The recording's lines seeded without their audio when a work is made on the device after a decode failure never count. The four-fifths threshold of decision A applies to this total.

## 2026-09-27: One wording, "four fifths" (CoS decision D)
The privacy page and the Seal both say a listen is "a work heard for at least four fifths of its length"; neither says 80%. The code's `HEARD_SHARE` stays 0.8, and the paired tests hold both pages to the wording.

## 2026-09-27: The kill threshold stays at 300 (CoS decision E)
`chapter_rendered` now counts listens heard to four fifths (decision A), not renders completed. The threshold stays at 300 until the first G4 recalibrates it. CHARTER.md's kill criteria carry one line saying what the count means, and contract.yaml's comment on the event says the same.

## 2026-09-27: Nothing is ever unreachable; a panel may scroll inside itself (CoS decision F)
Chief of Staff decision, delegated by the founder, from the T8 review. When a panel's content cannot fit its screen, the panel scrolls vertically inside itself (`.panel` has `overflow-y: auto` and `overscroll-behavior: contain`), and the snap still moves between panels. The founder's brief allows vertical scrolling ("scroll to next page if needed but not right or left"); clipping is not allowed. Fitting comes first: at short heights secondary text goes (the per-line count, the loudness note, the 10 s keys, the station line on a Broadcast, Keep it's size lines), and the panel scroll is the safety net. Widgets use `overflow: clip`, so focus can never scroll one by itself and strand its content; long text scrolls in its own scroller. At the panel map's six viewports everything still fits unscrolled.

## 2026-09-27: npm audit's sharp findings are an accepted risk
Chief of Staff decision, from the pre-Proof audit (T9). `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 3 high findings: libvips and libheif CVEs in `sharp`, reached through `@huggingface/transformers` under `kokoro-js`, with no fixed version. sharp is Node image-processing code: it is installed, never loaded by anything Dial ships. The browser build replaces it with the bundler's empty "sharp (ignored)" stub (tests/pre-proof.test.ts checks every built script), Dial processes no images anywhere, and the Worker does not import it. Accepted until a fixed transformers release exists; re-checked by 2026-10-11 and at every transformers or kokoro-js bump.

## 2026-09-27: T9 hardening from the pre-Proof audit
Chief of Staff scope, built in T9. Every response carries `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000` (no preload until the zone's "Always Use HTTPS" is on and has held) and `Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=(), usb=()`; the http to https redirect is the zone setting, the founder's to switch on, not the Worker's. POST /e and POST /feedback get their own rate-limit bindings, `RL_EVENTS` (6 per 60 s, namespace 7302) and `RL_FEEDBACK` (2 per 60 s, 7303), beside `RL_API` (7301, everything else); Rollbook holds 7201 to 7203. A refused request gets a 429 with `retry-after`, `{"error":"rate_limited","message":...}` in plain words. Any failure inside the Worker (D1 past its read cap) answers a 503 in JSON with the security headers, never an error page or a stack trace. Feedback stores its `page` only when it is a site path (`^/[a-z0-9/-]{0,64}$`).
The model's cache is named for its revision and its SHA-256 (`dial-voice-<revision>-<sha256[0:16]>`), so a changed pin downloads it again. Anyone who listened on release/2026.09.26-2 downloads the model once more; no saved work is stranded, since offline saving has not yet shipped to production. A failure inside the Worker is a 503 (decision L below). The offline helper keeps only the core shell at install (about 0.6 MB: pages, main script and styles, Latin fonts, icons) and the rest (the render worker's script and extended-Latin faces, about 2.5 MB) when a work is saved; the privacy page says so. From the T9 review: a work can be saved through the old helper after a new one has installed its core, so saving asks the waiting helper too, and a helper that activates while a work is saved first completes its own shell; if it cannot (offline), it keeps the older builds' shells and answers a shell file its own lacks from them (`web/src/offline/shell-cache.ts`, tests/offline-update.test.ts runs that order). A render worker whose file name changed between the two builds is still missing offline until the connection returns.

## 2026-09-27: verify-production runs the deployed release's contract
verify-production read main's contract.yaml, which describes routes main has and the live release may not (/seal's 404, /play/*, /sw.js), so it would stay red on a healthy site. It now asks production's /healthz for its build, reads that release tag's contract.yaml (`scripts/deployed-contract.mjs`), and runs it with main's runner; a build that is not a release tag fails by itself. The one failed run (36271163902, 20:55Z on 2026-09-26) failed every check with "fetch failed" before any HTTP answer, and release/2026.09.26-2, which attached dial.voidvision.org, was cut at 22:09Z: the host was most likely not yet serving then, and a WAF block would have been a 429, not a failed fetch. The runner now sends a named User-Agent, retries once after a failure below HTTP, and prints its cause (DNS, refused, TLS), so the next red run says why. The workflow creates its `prod-red` label before opening an issue, which was the second failure in that run.
The audit's other performance finding, that the zone's WAF rule counts static files so a quick first listen on a shared IP can be blocked, stays open: the fix is the founder's zone rule (exempt /voice/*, /ort/*, /works/*, /recordings/*, /icons/* and /play/*, then contract.yaml's zone_waf_rule and privacy.html to match). The leaner shell above removes about 13 of the requests a first visit made.

## 2026-09-27: http:// must answer 301 to https:// (CoS decision I)
Chief of Staff decision, from the T9 review. contract.yaml checks that http://<host>/ answers 301 with a Location starting https:// (`type: redirect`, `requires: deployed`). Only the zone's host can pass it, so it carries `host_suffix: voidvision.org`: on a workers.dev preview (outside the zone) or localhost the runner reports it exactly "n/a (production host only)", neither passed nor skipped, and a /release preview is not blocked by it (T9 re-review). The redirect is the zone's "Always Use HTTPS" setting, so the check fails until the founder switches it on (the Board ask is open), and its failure message says so (`failure_note`). G3 requires it to pass.

## 2026-09-27: The write paths key IPv6 by /56 (CoS decision J)
Chief of Staff decision, from the T9 review. RL_EVENTS and RL_FEEDBACK key an IPv6 client by its /56 (a common home delegation holds 256 /64s to rotate through); RL_API stays /64; IPv4 stays the whole address (`worker/src/guard.ts` LIMITER_V6_PREFIX). Nothing is stored either way.

## 2026-09-27: A refused count is retried once; shared networks may undercount (CoS decision K)
Chief of Staff decision, from the T9 review. When /e answers 429, the page tries that one count once more after the answer's Retry-After (1 to 120 s, else 60), reading the counts switch again first; a second refusal is not retried, and a timer dies with the tab, so a queued retry is dropped when it closes (`web/src/telemetry.ts` sendEvent). CHARTER.md's kill criteria say counts may undercount on large shared networks and that a spike at the daily ceiling is suspect. The ceiling flag needs no new write: `event_counts` has no per-day flag column, and the counter stops exactly at its ceiling, so a day's row whose `count` equals `EVENT_DAILY_CEILING` is the flag. Adding a column or a server-only event was skipped, since either would add D1 writes for what the count already shows.

## 2026-09-27: The Worker's failure answer and its one log line (CoS decision L)
Chief of Staff decision, from the T9 review. A failure inside the Worker answers 503 `{"error":"unavailable","message":"Dial's counter is resting. Try again later."}` with no Retry-After, since a read cap lasts until the day turns. It logs one fixed line, `dial worker: unavailable (<error.name>)`, and nothing else: never the message, stack, request, body or address.
